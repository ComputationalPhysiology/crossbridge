# Tran2010

[Rice2008](rice2008.md) with a crossbridge cycle that is reversible, thermodynamically constrained and
sensitive to the products of ATP hydrolysis, from {cite}`tran2010metabolite`. Rice et al.'s rates
become pseudo-first-order rates at reference conditions (MgATP 5 mM, MgADP 36 µM, Pi 2 mM, pH 7.15),
and the metabolites act by mass action where they bind:

- **Pi** on the reverse of attachment (`XBprer → P`), so that it lowers force;
- **H+**, the proton released by hydrolysis, on the reverse of the powerstroke (`XBpostr → XBprer`),
  and **MgADP** between two force-bearing substates of `XBpostr`, which slows both steps leaving it;
- **MgATP** on detachment of the post-rotated bridge, where ATP is hydrolysed;
- a reverse of that step, `P → XBpostr`, whose rate the free energy of MgATP hydrolysis fixes
  (minute: 1.3e-10 /s at the defaults);
- **H+ competing with Ca2+** at troponin C, which slows calcium binding and so lowers calcium
  sensitivity.

The model is parameterized for guinea pig (Rice et al.'s rates slowed five-fold, `xbmodsp = 0.2`).
Metabolites are parameters, `MgATP`, `MgADP` and `Pi` in mM and `pH`, fixed for the model's lifetime:
build a new model to change them. `get_atp_hydrolysis_rate()` gives the net flux of the MgATP step per
myosin head.

**Reading the paper.** The class follows the Physiome Model Repository's CellML (`tran_2009`, curated
by Catherine Lloyd and licensed [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/)) in its
equations and parameters, also where the paper's Appendix differs:

- In the CellML, bridges that detach (from `XBprer` with Pi, from `XBpostr` with MgATP) go to the
  non-permissive state `N`, and the reverse step takes them from `P` without `P`'s equation losing
  them. The Appendix returns them to `P`, as Rice2008 does. The figures' steady forces differ between
  the two by at most 1.4%.
- The duty fractions' denominator has one term twice (`alpha1- alpha3-`) and lacks `alpha1- alpha2-`,
  and the maximal pre-rotated occupancy, which only scales the apparent troponin occupancy, has the
  temperature-scaled `hbT`. The Appendix prints both the same way.
- Temperature: the CellML gives the rates at 310 K, so the class at 36.85 °C (Rice2008 at 37 °C);
  `TmpC` defaults to the CellML's 299 K, 25.85 °C. Titin's rest length is the CellML's 1.85 µm.

**The exception is the proton concentration.** The CellML computes it as `10^7 / 10^pH` µM, ten times
[H+], against a dissociation constant of 0.02 µM, the paper's 2e-5 mM. The class uses [H+]. The paper's
Fig. 2B decides between them: the Ca50 at pH 7.5, 7.2, 6.8 and 6.4, relative to pH 7, is 42, 68, 151
and 363% with [H+], on the paper's curve (about 48, 70, 140 and 355); 32, 63, 160 and 409% with the
CellML's value. Elsewhere only `H / H_ref` enters, except the minute reverse rate, ten times smaller
than the CellML's, which changes a twitch by less than 1e-8.

**What reproduces.** Isometric steady states at saturating calcium reproduce the paper's figures to
about what can be read off them: force against Pi (Fig. 2A: 73.5, 58.1, 48.1, 41.0 and 31.6% at 5-30
mM, the paper about 72, 57, 49, 42 and 33), maximal force and Ca50 against pH (Fig. 2D), the MgADP
reversal point (Fig. 3A: the curves at 1 nM and 10 µM MgADP cross at 0.82 mM MgATP, the paper 0.9),
the steady force after redevelopment against MgATP (Fig. 3B: 0.518 and 0.239 of the force at 0.1 mM,
the paper 0.52 and 0.25), and maximal force through 15 min of ischemia (Fig. 5: 0.851, 0.334, 0.193,
0.090 and 0.059, the paper about 0.87, 0.34, 0.20, 0.08 and 0.05). The paper's absolute forces in Fig.
2D are 1.44 times the model's at every pH. What does not reproduce: the text's 13% rise of isometric
force from 0 to 3 mM MgADP, by which the MgADP dissociation constant was fitted; the model more than
doubles (×2.28), as does the paper's own Fig. 5 (row C, T = 0). Fig. 2C (maximal force against pH) is
below the paper's curve at its ends, by 11 and 8% of the reference force (131 and 30 at pH 8
and 6, the paper about 142 and 38). `tests/test_tran2010.py` pins each
model value; the docstring lists them.
 `demo/reproduce_figures_tran2010.py` plots all of these, together with tension
redevelopment after detachment (Fig. 3B-C) and the complex stiffness (Fig. 4: the shape reproduces, the
phase peaks are lower than the paper's).

**Stepping and force scale** are Rice2008's: the same integrator (exact block-wise exponentials of the
frozen-rate system over sub-steps of at most 0.1 ms, more while the length moves fast), the same guards
(the exponent cap of `hfmd` and `gxbmd`, the overlap floor, the calcium clamp), and outputs in units of
`Ta_max` (default 1.0, the paper's normalized force, not kPa). `Ka` has Rice2008's form.

**As an `.ode` file.**
[`tran2010.ode`](https://github.com/ComputationalPhysiology/crossbridge/blob/main/src/crossbridge/tran2010.ode),
reachable as `Tran2010.ODE_FILE`, is this model as a gotranx file, written to the contract in
{ref}`ode-files`, with the metabolites as parameters. `tests/test_ode_files.py` pins it to the class:
the outputs at 1 ms steps agree with a tight `solve_ivp` solution of the file to 1e-4 over Rice2008's
protocols and with every parameter off its default, the metabolites included, except a quick release
(1.2e-3, the class's integrator error, which shrinks with its sub-step); all nine states are compared,
and the right-hand sides agree to 1e-12 at random states that reach each branch.
`tools/compare_tran2010_cellml.py` downloads the CellML, corrects its proton concentration, and checks
the file against it at 200 random states, temperatures and metabolite levels (agreement to 3e-13; it
exits 0 when every quantity agrees to 1e-12).
