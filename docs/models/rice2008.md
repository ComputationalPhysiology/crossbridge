# Rice2008

A cooperative thin filament with a crossbridge cycle that carries strain, from {cite}`rice2008approximate`:
calcium binds troponin C with a low or a high affinity (without and with strongly bound crossbridges
nearby), and regulatory units switch between non-permissive and permissive through a steep Hill
function of their calcium occupancy, which stands in for the cooperativity of neighbouring units.
Permissive units recruit crossbridges into a weakly force-bearing pre-rotated state, which
isomerizes into the post-rotated state (the powerstroke) and detaches. Each attached state carries
the mean strain of its bridges, driven by half the sarcomere velocity, so the transition rates feel
the velocity: compressed pre-rotated bridges isomerize faster, and post-rotated ones detach faster
either side of the powerstroke. Active force is the strain-weighted occupancy times the thick-filament
single-overlap fraction, normalized to about 1 at full activation at 37 °C; temperature enters through
Q10 factors. `Rice2008.rabbit_parameters()` is the paper's rabbit variant. Its calcium activation is
the one {cite}`tewari2016influence` couples to a strain-dependent crossbridge cycle
([Tewari2016](tewari2016.md)); [Marzban2020](marzban2020.md), which extends Tewari2016, replaces that
activation with a cooperative thin filament and keeps only this model's collagen force.

The paper's muscle model (mass, viscosity, passive force, a series spring or an afterload) is not part
of the class: sarcomere length is an input, as in every model here. `get_passive_tension()` gives the
paper's passive force, titin and collagen. `demo/reproduce_figures_rice2008.py` couples the class to
that muscle model and reproduces the paper's figures.

**Reading the paper.** The paper, its CellML transcription (Physiome Model Repository) and the
authors' own code (the paper's Data S1) disagree in places. The class takes, in each case, the value
that agrees with Data S1:

- `kn_p = 500` and `kp_n = 50` /s, as the CellML has them; Table 1 prints them the other way round.
  With the table's values at most 9% of units would ever be permissive, while the force
  normalization (Eqs. 33-35) assumes `kn_pT >> kp_nT`.
- The sign of Eq. 22, which the paper's text loses, as in the CellML:
  `hfmd = exp(-sign(xXBprer) hfmdc (xXBprer / x_0)^2)`. Compressed bridges isomerize faster, as the
  paper's prose says.
- `SLrest = 1.9` µm, the paper's (it puts zero passive force there); the CellML has 1.85.
- `TmpC = 22.5` °C, the temperature of the paper's Figs. 5-7 and of Data S1; the CellML has 24.
- The collagen force and preload follow the paper's Eqs. 48 and 52.

`N_NoXB` and `P_NoXB` (Eqs. 7-8), the regulatory units outside the single-overlap region, are kept
as states, so that the states are Table 2's and the CellML's one for one. No output and no other state
depends on them.

The class departs from the paper in guards outside its range: the overlap length is floored at zero
(the formula turns negative below SL = 1.2 µm, under the paper's minimum of 1.4), calcium is clamped
at zero on input, and the exponent arguments of `hfmd` and `gxbmd` (Eqs. 22 and 24) are capped at 20.
The cap was set for the integrator, when it exponentiated the system as one matrix with a single
scaling exponent, which strain coefficients that grow without bound overscaled (with a cap of 40, a
50 µm/s stretch left the class 2.6 times the peak of `Ta` from the model, whatever its sub-step). The
step is now block-wise, and the cap stays as part of the model and of its `.ode`.
It binds only in fast lengthening (sustained above about 20 µm/s at 22.5 °C), where detachment is all
but instantaneous either way, and changes the outputs there by at most 1.1e-8 of their peaks.
Two expressions are evaluated in algebraically equal forms without a division that can be by zero
(Eq. 10, and the strain coefficients of Eqs. 29-30), and the permissive fraction is taken as
`Max(Tropreg, 0)` so that it cannot be NaN. The docstring has the details of the rest.

**Stepping.** `advance_step` takes any `dt`. It splits the call into sub-steps of at most 0.1 ms, and
into more while the length moves fast (at most 0.35 `x_0` of strain per sub-step), each an exact matrix
exponential of the linear system with its rates taken at a predicted midpoint, so it is second order.
The exponential is taken block by block (troponin, the units outside the overlap and the strains in
closed form, the 3 × 3 occupancies with a batched scaling and squaring), since with the rates frozen the
blocks share no entry; that is 27 times faster than one dense 10 × 10 exponential with numba and 4 times
without, and exact where the dense one lost the slow blocks to the stiff strain coefficients.
Each cell takes the sub-steps its own velocity needs, so its result does not depend on the other cells in
the batch. Against a Radau solution of the same equations, one 1 ms call agrees to 0.82% (22.5 °C) and
0.25% (37 °C) for shortening and lengthening at up to 300 µm/s. In sustained lengthening the class
converges to the model at every velocity measured (5-300 µm/s); at 1 ms calls its error is below 1e-3 of
the peak at most velocities and at most 1.5e-3, in narrow bands (around 44 µm/s at 22.5 °C; 13-20 and
35-47 µm/s at 37 °C). The paper's mechanics, as the demo runs them (at most 25.6 µm/s of shortening
and 4.96 µm/s of lengthening), stay well inside that range. The docstring has the measurements.

**What reproduces.** The model's equations (the CellML's, and so the `.ode`'s), solved with Radau at tight
tolerances, match the authors' own code to the precision of its sample outputs, the Fig. 5 twitch within
7e-6 and the F-Ca ramp within 2.5e-5; the class at 1 ms calls is within 2.1e-5 of that solution on the
twitch. The class reproduces the paper's steady states, twitches and tension redevelopment, mostly to
within what can be read off its figures, though not every printed value: Fig. 3B's Hill coefficient at
KSE 50 is 7.16 (printed 7.6), and Fig. 3C's pCa50 and nH are 5.98 and 2.77 (printed 5.9 and 3.0).
`tests/test_rice2008.py` pins each model value; the docstring lists them. Two things in the paper do not
follow its own model: Fig. 5's caption prints twitch durations (0.140, 0.187 and 0.223 s) that its
plotted curves contradict (they cross 50% at about 0.13, 0.16 and 0.21 s, where the model does: 0.1273,
0.1574 and 0.2150 s), and panel 5B was run at 2.2 µm, as the text says, not at the 2.3 µm of the
caption. Fig. 4, the force-velocity relations after quick releases, is not reproduced by the authors'
own code either; the demo reports where and by how much.

**Force scale.** `Ta`, `Ka` and `Tp` are the paper's normalized force (about 1 at full activation at
37 °C) times `Ta_max`, whose default is 1.0, and not kPa as for the other models. Pass the
isometric tension of the preparation as `Ta_max` to get stress, as with `RDQ18`; `Ka` is per unit
$\Lambda = SL/SL_0$ and leaves out the explicit dependence of the overlap on length, as the base class
defines it. `bound_calcium_fraction()` is the paper's apparent troponin occupancy (Eq. 37), which the
paper hands back to the cell model as buffering.

**As an `.ode` file.**
[`rice2008.ode`](https://github.com/ComputationalPhysiology/crossbridge/blob/main/src/crossbridge/rice2008.ode),
reachable as `Rice2008.ODE_FILE`, is this model as a gotranx file, written to the contract in
{ref}`ode-files`, with all nine states. `tests/test_ode_files.py` pins it to the class: the class's
`Ta`, `Ka`, `bound_ca` and `Tp` at 1 ms steps agree with a tight `solve_ivp` solution of the file to
1e-4 over the protocols of the moment models, a 50 µm/s stretch among them, with its parameters off
their defaults and with `rabbit_parameters()`. That is 1e-4 of each output's peak, or of `Ta_max` where
the peak is below it, as `Tp`'s always is and `Ta`'s is in all but the stretches. A slow stretch is
within 3.4e-4 and a quick release within a few 1e-3 (2.8e-3, 3.7e-3 with the rabbit parameters): the
tests' bounds, about 1.5 times the integrator's error, which shrinks with the class's own sub-step. A
separate test compares all nine states, `N_NoXB` and `P_NoXB` among them, and the right-hand sides
agree to 1e-12 at random states that reach each branch, both exponent caps and a negative calcium, with
the outputs compared at `Ta_max` 1 and 50. The file is also checked against the published CellML by
`tools/compare_rice2008_cellml.py`, which downloads the CellML and agrees with the file to 4.2e-14 at
200 random states (it exits 0 when every quantity agrees to 1e-12).
